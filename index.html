Guide

Orbital Watch shows every tracked object from the public NORAD catalog (via CelesTrak) and moves it in real time with the SGP4 orbit model.

Getting around

Click a dot to see what it is, where it is and where it goes next.
Star ★ an object to add it to your Watchlist and get alerts for it.
Layers choose which families of objects are drawn. Layers combine, so turn off “All active” to see only one family.
Legend (bottom) filters by orbit regime. Click a color to hide or show it.
Observer sets your ground site. It shows what is overhead now and predicts passes.
Conjunction finds objects that will come close to one of yours.

Orbit regimes

LEO Low Earth Orbit, below 2,000 km. ISS, Starlink, imaging satellites. One lap takes about 90 to 120 minutes.
MEO Medium Earth Orbit, 2,000 to 35,000 km. Navigation (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou).
GEO Geosynchronous, about 35,786 km. One lap per day, so these hover over one longitude. Used for TV, weather and communications.
HEO Highly elliptical. Swings close to Earth, then far out. Used for Molniya and transfer orbits.

Glossary

NORAD ID: catalog number of the object (ISS = 25544).
COSPAR ID: international designator (launch year, launch number, piece).
Apogee / Perigee: highest and lowest altitude of the orbit.
Inclination: tilt of the orbit relative to the equator. 90° passes over the poles.
Azimuth / Elevation: compass direction and angle above your horizon.
TLE: Two-Line Element set, the public orbit description. Older TLEs are less accurate.
Eclipse: the object is in Earth's shadow. Sunlit objects can be seen at dusk and dawn.
TCA / miss distance: time and distance of closest approach between two objects.
